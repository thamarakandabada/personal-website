I have written so many music-related posts over the years. They range from music recommendations, to gear reviews (these aren't directly music-related, but tangential), thoughts on music-adjacent films, gig recaps, and instrument talk. My knowledge may be limited, but that hasn't stopped me.

A few years ago I wrote [a post comparing the sound quality of Apple Music and Spotify](/notebook/2021/03/apple-music-vs-spotify). The comparison was done with music I intimately knew, so there is quite a bit here about the music I enjoy listening to. 

A few months ago I wrote [a post while waiting for a gig to start](/notebook/2026/05/waiting-for-big-thief). I’ve done that twice more since then.

A few weeks ago I wrote [a post about Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann](/notebook/2026/08/fleadh), an annual festival celebrating Irish music and culture, which was hosted in Belfast this year.

A few days ago I wrote [a post about how and why I started playing guitar](/notebook/2026/08/guitaring). I also did a [song exchange](/notebook/2026/08/lookahead) with [James](https://jamesg.blog/) which I really enjoyed.

And while I wouldn’t categorise these as blog posts, I always post a short write-up about every concert I go to on my [gigs page](/gigs).

A friend introduced me recently to someone with the phrase "has an encyclopaedic knowledge about certain areas of music". While I don't quite agree with my friend's assessment of me, I get why he would think that. I talk about music a lot, enthusiastically. Some years ago, I remember how another friend remarked on how I tended to talk about entire discographies and not singles. What knowledge I have on the subject comes from a place of deep love and appreciation. And I want to convey that in my writing, however impossible it may seem.
