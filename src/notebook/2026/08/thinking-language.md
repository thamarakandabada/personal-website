[Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language) is my native language. For the first few years of my life, growing up in Sri Lanka, it's all I spoke. Ours was a monolingual household—my parents didn't speak any other languages. Starting in kindergarten, and all the way through school, I learned English, as most other Sri Lankan kids do. I picked it up quite fast, which is more than I could say for the other national language of Sri Lanka, [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), which I never was any good at.

In my day-to-day, since we live in an English-speaking country, I mostly speak English. With my wife, at home, I speak in Sinhala generally, but we do switch between Sinhala and English every now and then. When we call our parents, we speak in Sinhala.

I can't remember when the switch happened, but somewhere along the way, I switched my "thinking language" from Sinhala to English. This may be difficult to understand for people who don't have an internal monologue—yes, [they exist](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2021/oct/25/the-last-great-mystery-of-the-mind-meet-the-people-who-have-unusual-or-non-existent-inner-voices)—but every single thought I have, every day, comes to me in English, unless I make a specific effort to think in Sinhala. My internal monologue is really quite eloquent, too, much more than I could ever hope to be in real life, but that's a topic for another day.


There must be some science behind this. A cursory Google (or DuckDuckGo, in my case) search brings up so many entries on this subject, but it's difficult to separate the wheat from the chaff. [This Reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/explainlikeimfive/comments/l6169v/eli5_what_language_do_bilingual_people_think_in/) was of some interest to me, where several multilingual people had shared their experience. I have no interest in laying down any theories in this post; I simply wish to pose the question for anyone reading who speaks more than one language. What language do you think in?
