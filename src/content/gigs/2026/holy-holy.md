I love doing this because it makes me feel close to the band, not just physically, but emotionally, too. It's hard not to feel emotional with these people on stage. It felt as if Tony was looking right at me the whole time, and Glen Gregory (of *Heaven 17* on vocals) was serenading me the whole night. I was close enough to feel the shockwave of air from the thunderous drums as Woody kept pounding on them like his life depended on it. Age has not slowed that man down.

At my [first Holy Holy gig](/gigs/2025/holy-holy/) in Liverpool last year, I was *adopted* by kind Bowie fans. We talked about music all night. This gig was no different. I was next to Barney-from-Warrenpoint and Dawn-from-many-places, both in their sixties, both massive fans. The conversation flowed effortlessly from David, to Lou Reed, to Iggy Pop, to T-Rex, to Stiff Little Fingers and Thin Lizzy, back to David. Barney is a guitar player, and we talked about our shared love for the instrument. Dawn had been following the band across the UK for a while. We traded favourite songs, albums, and bonded over how much David meant to us. We talked about how rock n' roll keeps you young.

The band aptly opened with *Five Years* (the first track on *The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars*) and went blisteringly into hit after hit. I loved the pacing of this set.

19. *Where Are We Now?*

Just as I did in Liverpool last year, I found myself getting quite emotional when *Rock 'n' Roll Suicide* came on. It has always been a favourite of mine. For me the definitive live version of the song is [this one](https://youtu.be/YnoyiVZUxUk) from 1990 in Tokyo. Every time David belts out "you're not alone" I can feel goosebumps. I would have given anything to be in that audience, but I was born too far away and too late. But today, with this audience in Belfast, and a year ago in Liverpool, I, along with everyone else in the room, cried my heart out in [collective effervescence](/notebook/2026/09/the-joy-of-live-music), just as that crowd in Tokyo would have all those years ago.

At the end of the gig, Glen, Tony, and Woody hinted at this being their last tour as the Holy Holy band. I hope they change their minds. I wish they would all live forever, as I know David's music will.
