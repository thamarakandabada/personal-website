For some time now, I’ve had a rule about not watching music-related biopics. They don't interest me in the least. I would happily watch a biopic of an activist, politician, author, painter, sports figure, or anyone else of note. Just not a musician. Especially not a musician I admire.

There is a lot of talk about Sam Mendes' upcoming Beatles biopics at the moment. You would think that as a massive fan of the band I would be first in line to watch them, but that couldn't be further from the truth. I'd rather these films didn't exist. (I am aware this is a very harsh stance to take). The Fab Four are being played by actors I admire. I've enjoyed all their work for years, but there is no actor I would watch playing these four people as characters on screen.

I've touched on this briefly in my [review](/notebook/2025/01/a-complete-unknown-an-incomplete-review) of James Mangold’s Bob Dylan biopic *A Complete Unknown* last year (which, ironically, was one of the few occasions when I broke this rule). I said in the post that factual accuracy, or lack thereof, is not what bothers me. There is no such thing as an accurate account of a public life, especially not in the music business. Stories get mangled in the press, are lost to time, and sometimes intentionally obfuscated. Take Bob Dylan for example. The mythology surrounding the man is far richer than an actual human life could ever be. Fans know that a lot of it is fabrication, sometimes perpetuated by the man himself. There is no true version of events for a life such as his. It's delusional to try and make one. The Beatles aren't any different.

I have my *own version* of my musical heroes' stories in my head. That's the only version that matters to me.
