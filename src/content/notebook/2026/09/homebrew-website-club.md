For those unaware, [Homebrew Website Club](https://indieweb.org/Homebrew_Website_Club) is a series of regular events hosted by the IndieWeb community where participants talk about personal websites. You don't need to be a coder to take part; the discussion is very beginner-friendly (although it *may* veer towards niche technical topics every now and then). The meetings are hosted by volunteers and take place on most Wednesdays. To cover the various time zones they are spread throughout the day.

I have been attending the Europe HWCs for the past few months. These events are virtual and are hosted by [James](https://jamesg.blog/). Other [online events](https://indieweb.org/Homebrew_Website_Club#Regular_Online_Meetups) include Asia Pacific, US Eastern, and US Pacific. There are regular [in person events](https://indieweb.org/Homebrew_Website_Club#Regular_In-person_Meetups), too, like the long-running HWC Nuremberg.

At HWC Europe, there is a group of regulars who are present most weeks, but we do get plenty of newcomers, too. There is no rigid structure for these meetings; we talk about what we have been making on our personal websites. Demos are shared and discussions are had. We also take notes of each meeting using [Etherpad](https://etherpad.org/), a collaborative note-taking software.

It's a great group of welcoming and supportive people from around the world. Yes, you don't need to be based in Europe to attend the Europe meetups. That part of the name simply refers to the timezone the event is hosted in. If the time works for you, you can just show up. (This applies to all online events).

I've demoed things I had made on this website at several meetings and received useful and encouraging feedback. It's always wonderful to see what other community members are making on their websites, too. I can trace back several choices I have made in the design and structure of my website to HWC demos I had seen. I hope I have inspired others with my demos, too.

Today's HWC was a bit different. James had asked me if I'd be interested in co-hosting, and I was happy to oblige. I helped by setting up the Etherpad and taking some notes during the event. I extended my laptop's display onto my TV so that I would have a dedicated screen to take notes on (see photo above). It worked well. The whole exercise was good fun; I'm already looking forward to the next event.

If you haven't been to one yet, please join us. I highly recommend it. All you need is an interest in personal websites and the independent web. We're all passionate about making the web more inclusive, accessible, and healthy. If you share this passion, you'll be right at home at HWC.
