After a lively discussion among some members of the [Web Writers' Collective](https://webwriterscollective.com/), a challenge was born. It involves a few food-related questions which participants are encouraged to answer on their websites. James' original post on this is linked above.

Here are my answers.

## What was your favourite food growing up?

I have trouble picking favourites when it comes to anything. If you had asked me this question back when I was a child, my answer would have been wildly different. But nostalgia is a funny thing, and I have no issues giving in to it completely. I'm going to pick [*Kiribath*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiribath) (කිරිබත්), a Sri Lankan rice dish.

The rice is cooked in coconut milk, giving it the name, with *kiri* (කිරි) in Sinhala meaning milk, and *bath* (බත්) meaning rice. The coconut milk gives it a rich, creamy texture. It is normally eaten with a garnish, such as *lunumiris* (ලුණු මිරිස්), literally meaning salt and chillies. I also like eating *kiribath* with a nice Sri Lankan curry (chicken or fish).

It is customary to serve *kiribath* cut into small squares or rhomboids. But some prefer to make a sticky, creamy version of the dish and serve it in spoonfuls. I prefer when it's cut into shape, but there is no right or wrong way.

## What is your favourite food that is made locally? (In your region/country.)

Sri Lankan street food is unique and fascinating, and [*Kottu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kottu) has always been a favourite of mine. It is made by mixing chopped [*roti*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti) with onions, chopped vegetables such as carrots and leeks, and chillies. An *egg kottu* will contain (scrambled) eggs, while a *chicken kottu* will have chicken pieces instead. There are several other versions, made with different kinds of meat such as pork and lamb. You get the idea.

Kottu is famously made in streetside eateries by mixing the ingredients together on a griddle using purpose-made iron blades, often by rhythmically pounding them. The music of the *kottu baas* (kottu maker) is a staple in any self-respecting Sri Lankan town. It is a clarion call for the hungry masses. A welcome commotion for the weary travellers. They shall be fed.

It is common knowledge in Sri Lanka that if you do not experience funny bowel movements the day after eating *kottu*, you haven't had the real deal. Keep this in mind if you ever try it.

## What is your favourite food generally?

It's hard to beat *kottu*. I will take it in any form, any time. Living in Northern Ireland, it's not always easy to get the ingredients required to bring out authentic Sri Lankan flavours in food, but I even like the inferior versions of *kottu* I've occasionally made using generally bland Tesco spices. I use tortillas as a *roti* substitute, and it works surprisingly well.

## What is one food you have had in the last year that you remember warmly?

In Cardiff earlier this year, we dined at a Thai restaurant called [*Giggling Squid*](https://www.gigglingsquid.com/). The food there was exceptional. Although I've tried and enjoyed Thai cuisine in many places before, I can safely say that this restaurant served the best Thai food I've ever tasted.

The papaya salad was a firm favourite. It had just the right amount of tang and heat, and worked great as an appetiser. We also tried a sharing platter of pork skewers, squid, spring rolls, and chicken wings. The [*pad thai*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pad_thai) with king prawns sealed the deal for me; I will remember this meal for a while yet. 

## What food do you really want to try?

I want to try more Caribbean cuisine. We had a small taste of it at a small restaurant in Aberystwyth called [*Kwizinn*](https://www.tripadvisor.co.uk/Restaurant_Review-g186428-d33396170-Reviews-Kwizinn-Aberystwyth_Ceredigion_Wales.html). I fell in love with the goat curry and the fried plantains. There is a whole lot more to explore. Options in Belfast are limited, but I hope the opportunity will come soon for us to dive into this amazing food culture again.

## Do you take photos of your meals?

I used to, back when I was a very active [Google Local Guide](https://maps.google.com/localguides/) posting reviews of every place I went to. I don't do it as much now, but not to worry, my wife is on the case. She has never seen a meal she hasn't taken a photo of.

## Savoury or sweet?

I prefer savoury, although I do have a bit of a sweet-tooth, too. Depending on the time of day, my mood, what else I've been eating that day, and what food had been on my mind, I would gladly take either.

## Spicy or no?

Spicy, please. Although, evidently, my spice tolerance seems to have dropped a bit after moving to Northern Ireland. My wife makes fun of this fact every chance she gets.
