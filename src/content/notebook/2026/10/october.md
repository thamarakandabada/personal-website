The PureGym app tells me that I have now visited them 6 times. That is about right—2 visits per week, over 3 weeks. We [said we'd go 3 times a week](/notebook/2026/10/sometimes-i-run-and-think/) but Belfast buses on Sundays are so infrequent that it's been impossible to do that so far. It is an excuse, I know, but I keep telling myself that things will change when we get a car. (That, too, is an excuse).

To keep the visits interesting, I've started to challenge myself to run faster. Outdoors, my best 5K time was 31 minutes and 35 seconds. On my 3rd visit to the gym, I ran a 5K in 21 minutes and 57 seconds. On my 5th visit, I got that down to 21 minutes and 18 seconds. The shorter runs suit me because it's no fun running while staring into a brick wall the whole time. Outside, I can vary my route when I feel like it. Even on the same route, things change—new people, new dogs, new obstacles. I run while taking in the sights, however boringly suburban and bourgeois they may be (being South Belfast), but the treadmill offers no such variety. I have a podcast in my ears but it is only a small relief.

All this is not to say that I have tired of the gym already. Trundling in after work is convenient, the facilities are reasonably clean and well maintained, and while it's busy, there are always treadmills available. You couldn't ask much more of a cheap gym in the city centre. If only I could learn to be comfortable in [boredom](/notebook/2026/04/boredom).
