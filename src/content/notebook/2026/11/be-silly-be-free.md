I've had the most extraordinary weekend. Yesterday I went with Nuwani to see [Westlife in concert](/gigs/2026/westlife), a group that was a big part of our childhoods. The short version of the story is this: we both used to attend a tuition class for English. This is very common in Sri Lanka—the English language is given such high status as a class signifier and a key to better economic opportunities, and children are encouraged to learn it from a young age. But I digress. The point is that we met at this class 18 years ago. The head teacher, an indefatigable man named [Kithsiri Jayasuriya](https://www.iesm.lk/leadership-messages/), in an attempt to introduce the students to English language pop music, found an outlet in the innocuous lyrics of Westlife. In a way, their music was the soundtrack of our young relationship, and seeing them perform live all these years later was a full circle moment for both of us.

Today we attended a [talk](/gigs/2026/bette-a-brian-eno) that was part of the [Belfast International Arts Festival](https://belfastinternationalartsfestival.com/), at which Brian Eno shared his views on the importance of art. It was a dignified event, attended by the kind of thoughtful audience who would take time on a Sunday afternoon to listen to a discussion about art. Brian, sporting his signature bemused wit, used silly filters on the videolink (pig ears, 'gangsta' sunglasses, and a graduation hat, among other things) drawing laughter from the crowd. It was not in any way denigrating the subject matter or the goodwill of the audience. The whole time, he spoke eloquently and intelligently, but had the good humour to make fun of himself. Here was a man who had achieved successes many could only dream of in multiple creative pursuits, but he didn't take himself too seriously. The talk itself touched on the subject of the needless intellectualisation of capital-A-*Art* that ends up excluding the everyday creativity of people, so his behaviour was entirely appropriate. A man with such impressive accomplishments and no artifice. As human as the rest of us.

Two seemingly unrelated events, you may think, but my mind is weaving a thread between the two thanks to a few conversations I've been in the middle of in the last day or two. In them, my sexuality has been all but called into question, albeit supposedly jokingly, in a harmless-workplace-banter sort of way, for being a fan of Westlife. By extension, it has also been insinuated that cheesy pop music is somehow a lower form of art, to be passively consumed, not passionately enjoyed. Normally, I would have dismissed these without a second thought, but it has tickled me just enough this time to write something down.

My first thought was a sense of relief for not having grown up inculcated in hyper-masculine drivel. It was around, as it ever is, but I thankfully did not subscribe to it. My tastes have not been coloured by any considerations of my masculinity or sexuality hinging on them. I like the things that I like unapologetically. I am realising that the people in my life that I love and admire, my friends with whom I share all parts of myself without reservation, have the same childlike curiosity free of judgement. They may have wildly different interests—some do art, some are ardently political, others live for sport—but a deep and ferocious passion binds them all. My people wear their convictions well, and don't apologise for them.

I've also been thinking about how the company I find myself to be most comfortable in, regardless of whether they are old friends or virtual strangers I met only minutes ago, consists of people who display genuine interest in others. The dullest people, to me, are those who cannot accept anyone of different circumstances or backgrounds without exoticisation or judgement.

I've never thought of myself as a particularly silly, or even playful, person. But everyday life has a way of beating playfulness out of you, so I think it is important now more than ever to accept that enjoying things is okay. Do things you love—go enjoy nature, take a walk on a rainy day, dance a little bit, play with a dog, make faces, laugh with a friend, sing loudly, wear a silly hat—and be grateful that you can, because not everyone has the luxury.

Be silly-ly, unapologetically, freely, yourself.
