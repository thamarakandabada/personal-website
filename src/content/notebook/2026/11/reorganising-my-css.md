Having put it off for far too long, I've finally reached that point where I can no longer ignore a certain task on the Trello board I maintain for my website: reorganising my CSS.

Some months ago, [João](https://jlopes.eu/) shared an article on the [IndieWeb chat](https://indieweb.org/discuss) that outlined a new way of organising global style sheets in a modular manner. The method, called [CSS Nouveau](https://www.spicyweb.dev/css-nouveau/), was popularised by [Jared White](https://jaredwhite.com/). I have to admit that in the beginning it intimidated me a little; after all, I'm new to all this. Yes, I did write the CSS for this website by hand from the ground up, but it was all an utter mess—a soup of `div` tags, held together delicately by classes upon classes of selectors. I was afraid that if I touched them, things would break.

Nothing of note happened that finally convinced me to jump in. I just sort of decided this morning that it was time.

Jared's course is certainly not aimed at beginners. It presupposes a certain level of knowledge and familiarity with CSS basics. I sometimes have to take a breather and go look things up elsewhere—[MDN docs](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/) are excellent for this—but I've been making steady progress so far.

Mine is only an inconsequential personal site with a very limited audience of friends and family, but the thought of building a "design system" for it interests me deeply. I like a project. I like putting my energy into crafting something with care. I don't think about who is, or isn't, going to see it. I do it for me.

I'm not looking for any feedback, but if you'd like a peek behind the scenes at what I've done, take a look at the [`styles` section](https://github.com/thamarakandabada/personal-website/tree/main/src/styles) in my source code. Better yet, write some CSS yourself. It's fun!
