My [blogroll](/blogroll) has grown in leaps and bounds over the last year. And I'm glad. Every day, over my lunch break, I catch up on the latest posts from people around the world that I follow. I don't get to all of them, of course, but between [haikus from Scotland](https://jamesg.blog/), [coffee shop reviews from Japan](https://www.emgoto.com/), [cycling adventures from Italy](https://jeremycherfas.net/), [weeknotes from Mexico](https://joelchrono.xyz/), [honest thoughts on money saving from Sri Lanka](https://rothbournewrites.substack.com/), and [helpful tech tips from the States](https://tantek.com/), I get to quite a lot.

I have been using [Current](https://www.currentreader.app/) as my RSS reader for a few months now, and I adore it. There are many others like it, but this is the one I like.

I first came across it on the developer Terry Godier's blog. He had written an impassioned [love letter to open web protocols](https://www.terrygodier.com/the-boring-internet) (of which RSS, naturally, is one) which someone had shared on a forum I frequent. I downloaded the app and never looked back.

The iOS and macOS apps keep my feed in sync. The UX is intuitive. I like the typography and the colour scheme. They're great apps overall.

It's not just people I follow. News outlets get the same treatment. Seeing the web through an RSS reader is a far more calming experience than the one a browser offers. I would, on occasion, leave my reader to browse around a fellow human's quirky personal website, of course, but I find that newsreading via RSS exerts less strain on the old noggin. On Current, I have my feed divided into sections: People, News, Sri Lanka, The Web, Film, Tech, Longform, and Self (to test my own RSS feeds). News articles disappear within 18 hours; the others stay in the feed indefinitely until I bid them adieu.

I should perhaps mention two other RSS-related projects that I admire. James' reader [Artemis](https://artemis.jamesg.blog/), and Pablo Enoc's aggregator [powRSS](https://powrss.com/). Go check them out and support the creators if you can. And finally, if you aren't already, consider following my updates via RSS—I have many [feeds](/feeds).
