If you missed it, here’s what I did. I wrote a story about doing unimportant things for 15 years to get to where I am today, and even though I thought those years were wasted, how they weren’t. (Yes, [this one](/notebook/2017/10/reality-check/).)

Then I ran a poll on Instagram. I asked if I should write more like this. Put another way, I simply asked for your approval to vent more often. There were 10 yes-es, and 1 no.

I’m going to take that a little seriously and write more often about things that go on inside my head. Look out for these and share your feedback with me :\)

Now, for the goodies.

## 1\. I recommended [Basecamp](https://basecamp.com)'s “The Distance” podcast in my last Letter. There’s another Basecamp podcast I want you to try out.

Basecamp ended _The Distance_ in August 2017, and started a new one in its place. _Rework_ is the name.

[_Rework_](https://rework.fm) is all about (unconventional) management wisdom from Basecamp’s founders, [Jason Fried](https://medium.com/u/c030228809f2) and [DHH](https://medium.com/u/54bcbf647830), two of my business idols. You needn’t worry if business management isn’t your cup of tea. This show is far more wide-raging, and utterly insightful.

## 2\. Forget “Management.” Philosophy is where it’s at.

So says Matthew Stewart in [this delightful feature](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2006/06/the-management-myth/304883/) he wrote for The Atlantic back in 2006. I think it hasn’t lost its relevance the slightest, even after 11 years.

<blockquote>Most of management theory is inane. If you want to succeed in business, don’t get an M.B.A. Study philosophy instead.</blockquote>

## 3\. But, where does one study Philosophy?

If you’ve read through Stewart’s argument, and are itching for some philosophy, there are many great sources on the interwebs where you can indulge. I would start with some free courses offered by [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org) and [edX](https://www.edx.org).

If you prefer a much more concise and preliminary introduction to some of the most sought after thinkers in the history of philosophy, [this book](https://www.amazon.com/Philosophy-Essential-Thinkers-Philip-Stokes-ebook/dp/B00GO6TMFS/ref=tmm_kin_swatch_0?_encoding=UTF8&qid=&sr=) would help. The Kindle edition is only $0.99.

## 4\. Speaking of books, do you read a lot?

If you do, I’d imagine you spend a fortune on books every year? I certainly do. But a few months back I found this website, and now I spend far less.

[BookBub](https://www.bookbub.com/) lets you subscribe to deal alerts for books you’re thinking of buying. You can follow the book titles, or the authors. When there is a deal announced somewhere (think: Kindle, Apple iBooks, etc.) they will send you an email notification.

## 5\. Power corrupts. PowerPoint corrupts absolutely.

To end on a lighter note, there is [this essay that I read on WIRED](https://www.wired.com/2003/09/ppt2/) a while back which was quite hilarious, and made a lot of sense at the same time. (By the way, this has nothing to do with my personal preference for Keynote over PowerPoint as an app).

In this short essay, [Edward Tufte](https://www.edwardtufte.com/tufte/) argues how PowerPoint presentations (meaning, graphical presentations in general, regardless of the app used to create them) have become an excuse instead of a complement.
